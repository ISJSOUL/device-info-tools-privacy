Device Info & Tools

Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 October 2026

Device Info & Tools reads information about your phone and shows it to you. That information stays on your phone. There is no account, no server of ours, and nothing about your device is uploaded to us — because there is nowhere for it to be uploaded to.

This policy is specific rather than general: below you will find the exact permissions the app requests, what each one is for, and every internet address the app is capable of contacting.

What stays on your device

Everything the app reads about your hardware is processed locally and never transmitted: processor, memory, storage, battery, temperatures, display, cameras, sensors, network configuration, installed applications, and the results of every hardware test.

Hardware test results are saved on your phone so you can see what has already been checked: the verdict, a short explanation, and the measurements behind it. They stay there until you run the test again or uninstall the app.

Reports you export as text or PDF are written to your device's own storage. They are shared only if you choose to share them, with whichever app you choose.

The full checkup can produce a PDF inspection certificate, intended for showing to someone buying or selling a phone. It contains the phone's model and Android version, the date, and each check with its verdict. It deliberately leaves out the IMEI, serial number and the owner's details. It is created only when you ask for it, and shared only if you choose to share it.

The certificate's code repeats those results with a signature, so anyone scanning it with this app can see whether the PDF was edited. It also carries an eight-character code worked out one way from the identifier Android gives this app on this phone. That lets a buyer, scanning with the app on the phone itself, confirm the certificate was made on that phone. The code cannot be turned back into any identifier, no other app can produce it, and a factory reset changes it. Verifying a certificate happens entirely on the phone doing the scanning.

The QR code scanner and generator work entirely on the phone. What a scanned code contains is shown to you and kept nowhere; a link in it opens only if you tap Open link, and a Wi-Fi password is copied only if you tap Copy password. Codes you create are drawn on the phone and leave it only if you share them.

Dead Zone Mapper. It maps one of three things, room by room. Wi-Fi reads the strength, link speed and band of the Wi-Fi network you are connected to - not its name. Mobile signal reads the strength and type (4G/5G) of your mobile signal - not the network, the cell or your location. Sound measures how loud each room is with the microphone; the sound is turned into a level as it arrives and is never recorded or saved. No location permission is used. Map my walk draws your route from the phone's motion sensors - the step detector or accelerometer, the gyroscope, the compass and, where there is one, the barometer: it counts your steps, notes which way you walk and whether you change floor, and plots them as distances from where you started. It is not a location - no GPS, no map coordinates - and the sensors are read only while you are mapping. You can draw rooms, walls and labels on the map; they are kept with the survey. Surveys and their maps stay on the phone. Save puts a picture of the map in your Pictures folder; a map or report leaves the phone only if you share it.

Live guard. If you turn it on, a small background service watches the battery temperature and Android's own overheating signal as they change, and every 15 minutes checks whether charging is working, how fast the battery falls while the screen is off, and how full storage and memory are. It warns you with a notification when something needs attention. Android requires it to show a notification of its own while it runs. Everything it reads is used on the phone at that moment and is never sent anywhere; the only thing kept is which alerts you have already seen or snoozed, so they are not repeated. You can turn it off at any time from its notification or in More.

Battery recording. Battery Detective and Charge Forecast learn from a record of your battery, which is off until you switch it on in either tool. While it is on, the app notes, every 15 minutes, every 2 minutes while charging, and whenever a charger is plugged in or out: the battery level, whether it is charging and the charger type and power, the battery temperature, current and voltage, whether the screen is on, and the mobile signal strength (a number in dBm - not the network, the cell or your location). The record is kept on the phone for two weeks, is never sent anywhere, and can be deleted at any time with Delete recorded history in Battery Detective. Switching recording off stops it.

App usage (Battery Detective). If you choose to allow Usage access in your phone's settings, Battery Detective reads Android's record of which apps were on screen, and which kept running a foreground service while the screen was off, during the time battery recording covers (up to the last seven days). It uses this only to show which apps were active when the battery fell, as app names and times on your screen. It is read on the phone each time you open Battery Detective, is never saved by the app, and is never sent anywhere. You can take the access back at any time in Settings › Usage access.

The storage capacity check, when you start it, fills the phone's free space with test data and reads it back, then deletes it - including after an interruption, the next time the app opens. The test data is meaningless filler and never leaves the phone.

Every internet address the app can contact

The app does not upload device reports to any server we operate. The tools below contact their addresses only when you use them. Two things happen without you opening a tool: when the app starts or comes back to the front it checks your optional Premium purchase with Google Play, and, unless you have Premium, it starts the advertising service described below.

  • speed.cloudflare.com Download and upload speed measurement, only when you tap Run Speed Test
  • api.ipify.org Returns your public IP address for the Network screen
  • ipapi.co Returns your public IP with the approximate city and internet provider that address is registered to
  • whois.iana.org WHOIS registry lookup, only for a domain name you type in yourself
  • google.com A connection test used to measure round-trip latency
  • any host you enter The DNS lookup, port scanner, and ping tools connect to whatever address you type into them
  • Google Play services and the Play Store Checking your optional Premium purchase and its local prices when the app starts or comes back to the front
  • Unity LevelPlay, Unity Ads and Meta Audience Network servers Choosing and delivering the advertisements described below. Meta’s are at facebook.com addresses.
On IP address lookups. Any service that tells you your public IP address necessarily sees that address, and ipapi.co also returns the approximate city and provider it is registered to. This is how the feature works, not something happening behind it. If you would rather not contact those services, avoid the Public IP card on the Network screen.

Advertising

The app displays advertisements through Unity LevelPlay, Unity Technologies’ advertising service, which asks two ad networks to supply them: Unity Ads and Meta Audience Network. To choose and serve an ad, LevelPlay and those networks collect your device’s advertising ID (or, where that is unavailable, an app-set ID), device and network information including your IP address, from which an approximate location can be inferred, and information about how ads are shown and interacted with, such as whether one was displayed or tapped. This is those companies’ collection, governed by their own policies, not ours — we receive no personal information from it.

  • Unity’s handling of this data is described in the Unity Player Privacy Policy, and Meta’s in the Meta Privacy Policy.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads. Deleting it stops the advertising ID being available to any app on the device.
  • What we tell the ad networks. This app never asks you to consent to personalised advertising, and never claims that you have. It tells LevelPlay, which passes it on to Unity Ads, two things about every user, in every country: that consent has not been given, and that the user is opted out of the sale or sharing of personal information. Unity Ads then serves only contextual advertising, chosen from the app you are using rather than from a profile of you.
  • What we tell Meta. LevelPlay does not pass those two signals on to Meta, so the app tells Meta directly that it should limit its use of data where US state privacy laws apply (Meta’s “Limited Data Use” setting, applied to residents of California, Colorado and Connecticut), and that the app is not directed at children. Beyond that, Meta applies its own rules in choosing which ads to show, as its privacy policy describes. We cannot see or control how it chooses, so we do not promise that the advertising Meta supplies is not personalised.
  • What Meta’s software looks at on your phone. It checks whether the Facebook app and a browser that supports Chrome Custom Tabs are installed, so that an advertisement’s link can open correctly. It does not read the list of the apps installed on your phone.
  • Android’s interest-based advertising features are switched off. The advertising software requests access to Android’s Topics and Attribution features, which support interest-based advertising and ad measurement. This app removes both permissions, so neither can be used.
  • Because the app does not ask for advertising consent, there is no consent prompt to show you and no consent choice stored on your device.

Where advertisements appear

Banners, in two kinds of place: within the list of hardware tests, and at the foot of an information or tool result screen. You never see more than one at a time, each is labelled Advertisement, space for one appears only when there is an advertisement to show, and how often a new one may be requested is capped.

A full-screen advertisement between screens. When you leave a tool result or the Device health report after reading it, going back may show one full-screen advertisement, which you can close. It never appears when you open a tool or the report, never while the report is still being prepared, never more than once every two minutes and never more than ten times a day.

There is no advertisement when the app opens, none when it closes, none during a hardware test, none over a permission request, and none over a screen you are reading.

The video advertisement, and what it gives you

A short video you choose to watch, never one that starts by itself. It is offered in two places: on the More tab as No ads for 15 minutes, and when opening Terminal or Export report, which it pays for if you have not removed ads:

  • Watching one to the end removes all advertising for the next 15 minutes, and opens Terminal or Export report if that is where you were. The app stores the moment that period ends, on your device only, so it survives closing the app.
  • Declining leaves that one feature closed, and changes nothing else. Every information screen, every tool and every hardware test stays fully available without watching anything.
  • If no video is available — no connection, or none ready to show — the feature opens anyway. You are never held up by an advertisement that failed to appear.
  • While that 15-minute period is running you will not be offered another video, and both features open straight away.
  • Removing ads opens both permanently, with no videos at all.

Removing advertising permanently

The Premium purchase removes all advertising for good. When that is active the advertising SDK is not started at all, so no advertising ID is collected for it — see Premium and in-app purchases below.

Premium and in-app purchases

The app offers one optional one-time Premium purchase (not a subscription). It unlocks, permanently: the Premium tools (Dead Zone Mapper, Battery Detective and Charge Forecast), Terminal and Export report without a video, and no advertising. Every other information screen, tool and hardware test is free to everyone.

  • Payments are handled by Google Play. They are processed entirely inside Google’s billing system. We never see, receive or store your card number, billing address, or any other payment detail.
  • What the app asks Google, and why. On launch the app asks Google Play whether this account already owns one of the Premium products. It uses that single yes-or-no answer for one purpose: to unlock Premium, which also switches advertising off. Nothing else is read from your Play account.
  • What is stored on your device. The answer is cached locally so that someone who bought Premium never sees a banner flash or a locked tool while Google Play is being queried. It is a single true/false value, it never leaves your phone, and it is removed when the app is uninstalled.
  • No profile is built. We do not know who bought Premium, or when. There is no server of ours for that information to reach.
  • Google’s handling of a purchase is covered by the Google Privacy Policy and the Google Play Terms of Service. Refunds are requested through Google Play, not through us.

Children

This app is a technical utility and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Deleting your data

There is no account to close and no data held on any server we operate. Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored. That storage is limited to the following, all of it on your device only:

  • your theme preference;
  • the time at which an advertisement-free period earned from a video ends, and the time an advertisement was last shown, both used only to keep advertising infrequent;
  • whether Premium has been bought, as a single true/false value;
  • whether device tip notifications are switched on;
  • whether Live guard is switched on, and which of its alerts you have already seen or snoozed;
  • whether you have finished the welcome tour, so it is not shown again;
  • the results of hardware tests you have run, and how far through a checkup you are;
  • settings you give the measuring tools: the level's and ruler's calibration, the sound meter's adjustment, the height you hold the phone at for distance measuring, and a battery capacity you enter;
  • which steps you have ticked on the before-you-sell checklist;
  • your Dead Zone Mapper surveys: the room names you choose, the Wi-Fi strength, mobile signal strength or sound level measured in each, and, if you map a walk, the route drawn from it and any rooms, walls and labels you draw on it;
  • the battery record, only while you have it switched on, kept for two weeks, and the names you give your chargers - see Battery recording above;
  • a short rolling history of memory, battery and temperature readings, kept solely to draw the trend graph on the home screen widget.

To clear the advertising ID held against your device, use the Android setting linked above. Data already collected by Unity or Meta is subject to the privacy policies linked in the Advertising section, which describe how to contact them about it.
